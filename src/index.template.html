How it works

Your phone has no depth sensor it can use from a web page, so the app works out where the paper is by watching printed square tags in the camera image.

Why tags

Each tag is a square of known shape. Seeing one gives the browser enough geometry to work out how the paper is tilted and how far away it is, which is what lets the overlay sit on the page in correct perspective rather than floating on the screen.

You do not have to arrange them in any pattern. Scatter them, mix sizes, put them at odd angles. On the first sweep the app measures where each tag sits relative to the others and builds a little map of your desk, so afterwards any single tag in view is enough.

Getting steady results

  • Keep two tags in view when you can. Two tags pin the perspective properly. One tag leaves it to be inferred from four corners, and small measurement errors get amplified the further you look from that tag, so the image drifts by a few millimetres.
  • Spread the tags around the drawing, not clustered in one corner, so your hand never covers all of them at once.
  • Even, diffuse light. Glare on a tag hides it; hard shadow across one does too.
  • Matte paper. Glossy label stock reflects and blinds the tracker.
  • Bigger tags reach further. 20 mm for a sketchbook, 30 mm for A4 on a desk, 45 mm for an easel.
  • A phone stand or clamp helps enormously - not because the tracking needs it, but because your hands are then free to draw.

Trace styles

Neural models draw your photo entirely on your device (each style is a one-time download of 4-28 MB, then works offline). Artist sketch gives clean delicate lines, Rough sketch a loose underdrawing, Ink brush bold brush strokes. Paprika renders a painted version - a colour guide for painting what you traced. Ghost keeps the original photo, faded, for shading reference. Original shows it untouched - right for artwork that is already line art, like the built-in flowers.

Threshold trims faint pencil marks, and Thickness fattens the lines for bright conditions.

Getting it onto your phone

Browsers only grant camera access to pages served over https:// (or localhost). A file opened from your Files app does not qualify on iPhone, so this page needs to be served. Any of these takes about a minute:

  • Drag the HTML file onto app.netlify.com/drop - it hands you an https link instantly, no account needed. Open that link on your phone.
  • Put it in a GitHub repository and turn on GitHub Pages.
  • Serve the folder from your computer and expose it with a tunnel such as cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:8000.

On a desktop or laptop, opening the file directly usually does work in Chrome, which is handy for trying the styles out before you print anything.

Add it to your home screen once it's on an https link (Safari's Share sheet → Add to Home Screen). Opening it that way instead of as a browser tab drops Safari's own chrome and tab-switching overhead, which noticeably smooths out the camera tracking.

Privacy

Everything runs locally in the browser. No frames, images or measurements are sent anywhere, and the app makes no network requests at all once it has loaded.
